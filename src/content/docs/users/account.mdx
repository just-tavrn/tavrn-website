Your account is protected in two ways: a **second factor** keeps others from signing in, and your **recovery key**
lets you, and only you, read your encrypted messages on a new device. You find everything on this page under
**Settings → Security**.

<Screenshot scene="security-desktop" />

## The second factor

Every account on tavrn has a second factor, and nobody can switch it off, not even the operator of the node. You
choose one when you register and can add more later:

- an **authenticator app** on your phone that shows six-digit codes, or
- a **passkey**: your fingerprint, your face, a PIN or a security key.

<Screenshot scene="onboarding-desktop" />

You need the second factor every time you sign in with your password. Keep at least one; two are better, so that
losing your phone does not lock you out.

## Recovery codes

At registration you get ten **recovery codes**. Each of them lets you sign in once when your authenticator app or
passkey is not at hand. They are shown only once, so save them, for example in a password manager. If you used
some or think someone saw them, choose **Generate new codes**: the new codes replace all old ones.

## The recovery key

The **recovery key** is a list of 24 words (there is also a compact form of the same key). It unlocks your
encrypted messages when you sign in on a new device or in a new browser. Your browser creates it, and it is never
sent anywhere: neither the node nor its operator can restore it for you.

<Screenshot scene="onboarding-recovery-key-desktop" />

Because it is never stored, it cannot be shown again. If you lost it, or someone may have seen it, choose
**Create new recovery key**: your messages stay readable, and the old key stops working.

:::caution
Recovery codes and the recovery key are different things. The codes help you **sign in**; the key **unlocks your
messages**. Keep both.
:::

## Passkeys

A passkey can replace the password: **Sign in with a passkey** needs nothing else. Passkeys marked **unlocks
messages** can also unlock your encrypted messages on a new device, just like the recovery key.

**Settings → Security** says for every passkey whether it does:

- **unlocks messages**: ready.
- **can unlock messages: not enabled yet**, or **message unlock not confirmed**: choose **Enable message unlock**
  on a device whose messages are unlocked and confirm once with that passkey. This is needed for a passkey you
  added while the device was locked, and for authenticators that report the needed extension only when they are
  used.
- **cannot unlock messages here**: the passkey signs you in, but its authenticator cannot unlock messages. Most
  security keys can; Windows Hello can only on Windows 11 24H2 or 25H2 with the February 2026 update or newer. The
  button stays, so you can try again after a system update.

## Your devices

Every browser and app you sign in with is a separate device with its own keys. A new device asks for your recovery
key or a passkey once, then it reads your conversations. On a shared computer, turn on **Require the recovery key
or a passkey every time**: closing the browser then locks your messages on that computer. Devices you have not
used for a long time are removed by the node.

<Screenshot scene="settings-devices-desktop" />

Your **security key**, under **Settings → Security** as well, proves to others that your devices are yours. If
you fear someone saw it but still have it, rotate it: your contacts follow the new key without a warning.
