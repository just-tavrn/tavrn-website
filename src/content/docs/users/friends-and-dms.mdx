Direct messages and groups are always end-to-end encrypted. They work across nodes: you write to people by their
address, wherever their home node is.

## Friends

Open **Friends** on the home view and send a request to a username or a full address such as `bob@other.example`.
The other person accepts or declines. The same page lists incoming and sent requests, your friends and the people
you blocked.

<Screenshot scene="friends-desktop" />

## Direct messages and groups

Choose **New direct message** and pick a friend, or type a name or `name@domain`. **New group** starts a
conversation with several people; a group can have a name, and members can be added or removed later. A direct
message lives on the home node of the person who started it, and the list shows that node's domain.

## Who can message you

Under **Settings → Privacy & safety → Who can message me** you choose between:

- **Friends only**,
- **Friends and people in shared rooms** (the default), or
- **Everyone**.

Your node may allow less than you choose. Messages from people who are not your friends wait under **Message
requests**: you read the first message, then accept, decline, or decline and block. The sender is not told when you
decline.

**Block** someone from their menu or the friends page: they can no longer send you requests or messages and do not
see whether you are online.

## Verifying a contact

Every person has a **security key** that proves their devices belong to them. To make sure nobody is listening in,
open **Verify** in a contact's menu and compare the **safety number** with them in person or over a call, or let
them scan the QR code. If the numbers match, mark the contact as verified.

If the security key of a verified contact changes later, tavrn warns you and stops sending to them until you verify
again or confirm that you trust the new key. A changed key usually means that the person set up their account
again, but asking them is the safe thing to do.
